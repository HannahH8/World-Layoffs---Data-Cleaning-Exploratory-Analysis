This dataset ranges from the beinginning of COVID-19 onward. A time period of major economic and business disruption.

Key Insights:
1.) Rather than being isolated to COVID shutdowns, layoffs persisted into 2022–2023. With 2023 recording the largest number of layoffs. suggesting broader macroeconomic pressures such as:

- rising interest rates
- inflation
- overhiring during the tech boom
- slowing venture capital investment
- reduced consumer spending
- firms shifted focus from growth to operational efficiency
- investors prioritized profitability over expansion

2.) The companies with the largest layoffs were also major enterprises
  
Large firms often:
- overhire during growth periods
- scale aggressively
- carry higher labor costs
When economic conditions tighten, these companies conduct mass layoffs to:
- reduce operating expenses
- improve margins
- reassure investors

3.) Consumer Industry Experienced the Most Layoffs

Consumer-facing businesses are highly sensitive to:
- inflation
- changes in spending habits
- economic downturns
- supply chain disruptions
As consumer demand weakened after the pandemic surge, many businesses likely:
- reduced staffing
- cut expansion plans
- consolidated operations
This suggests that discretionary spending sectors were among the most vulnerable during the
economic slowdown.
